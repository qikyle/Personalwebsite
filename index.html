02 / Thesis

Why Chinese founders.
Why global markets.
Why now.

Three converging forces make this the most important moment to be investing at this intersection.

01

AI-Native from Day One

The best Chinese technical founders don't bolt AI onto existing products — they architect around it from the start. That changes the unit economics, the moat, and the ceiling.

02

Global Market Ambition

The founders I back are building for San Francisco and Singapore, not Shenzhen. They understand that the biggest TAMs are outside China, and they're willing to move to capture them.

03

Supply-Chain Leverage

For hardware and consumer products, Chinese supply chains offer a 3–10x cost advantage that US competitors can't easily replicate. That's a structural moat hiding in plain sight.

04 / What I Look For

The founders I back share a few things.

It's less about the deck and more about the person. I want to understand why you, why now, and why this — and I want the answer to be one I couldn't have predicted.

I prioritize depth over breadth. I'd rather have a small number of portfolio companies I'm genuinely useful to than a large number I barely know.

  • Chinese founding team Core technical or product background. One-line tech experience isn't enough — show me what you've actually built.
  • Product already live At minimum an MVP with real users or early customers. Preference for teams who've already found their first paying customer.
  • US-committed Building for global markets means committing to the hardest ones. I want founders who are in — or actively moving — to the US.
  • Seed to Series A I lead and participate in rounds from $500K to $5M. Too early I can't add enough; too late and I'm not the right partner.
  • AI-native or supply-chain edge Either AI is the product (not a feature), or your supply-chain access creates a cost advantage that compounds over time.
05 / How I Work

Fast opinions. Real support. Honest feedback.

First Meeting
One conversation, one week turnaround.
I commit to giving you a clear signal within a week of our first meeting — either moving forward, or telling you honestly why not. I don't ghost, and I don't run indefinitely slow processes.
Due Diligence
I go deep, not wide.
My diligence is product-first. I want to understand how your thing works, why it's better, and what breaks it — before we talk about market size or exit multiples.
Post-Investment
Available when it matters.
I don't pretend to know your business better than you do. But when you're facing a hard decision — hiring, pricing, co-investors, pivots — I want to be the first call, not the last.
Network
Warm introductions, not empty connections.
I make introductions selectively and specifically. When I connect you to someone, it's because I genuinely believe both sides will get something real from it.
06 / Background

Where I come from.

I grew up between cultures — which is part of why I find myself drawn to founders who are doing the same thing professionally. Building for a market that isn't the one you grew up in is hard, and I have a lot of respect for the people who choose to do it anyway.

Llama Ventures was founded on the conviction that the next generation of category-defining AI companies will be built by technical Chinese founders who understand both sides of the Pacific. I believe that more now than when we started.
